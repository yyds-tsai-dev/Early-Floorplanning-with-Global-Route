### Thread 2 — Fixed-outline floorplanning SOTA

**CSF / CSAQ** — nonsmooth analytic floorplanning solved by a conjugate subgradient algorithm (CSA), with a Q-learning layer that adaptively tunes the CSA step-size scaling constant per iteration (state = candidate solution, action = pick a stepsize scale from a small discrete set, reward = objective improvement / 100). Primary source: Meng, Cheng, Chen, Xu, Chen, Zhang, arXiv:2504.03796v2 (2025); peer-reviewed as *Electronics* 2025, 14(24):4893, DOI 10.3390/electronics14244893. Reported runtimes at MCNC scale (ami33 n=33, ami49 n=49) are sub-second; reported improvements are mostly internal ablations between the paper's own variants (0.1–1.7% HPWL), not clearly quantified head-to-head against Fast-SA/B*-tree baselines from the accessible text. **Applicability**: low — the representation is flat continuous-coordinate, incompatible with our `SequencePairEngine`/`BStarEngine` topology abstraction without replacing the placement-engine interface entirely, and the paper explicitly restricts itself to hard-modules-only (no soft-block aspect-ratio handling), which is core to our problem. The Q-learning-for-stepsize idea is a generic adaptive-parameter-control trick that Fast-SA's own adaptive cooling already covers without adding an RL dependency. **Payoff**: low. **Effort**: L (would require a new engine, not an augmentation). **Risk**: high (representation mismatch, unproven on soft blocks).

**FFA-CD / FA-GSS** — mixed discrete/continuous floorplanning: module orientation (0/90/180/270°) via a Distribution Evolutionary Algorithm on a Population Probability Model (DEA-PPM); continuous x/y via the same CSA family as CSF. Primary source: Sun, Cheng, Wu, Zhu, Chen, arXiv:2401.15317v1 (2024), DBLP `abs-2401-15317` (no separate peer-reviewed venue found). Quantitative claims ("superior to some celebrated B*-tree based floorplanning algorithms") could not be extracted from the accessible full text — flagged as **unverified**. **Applicability**: low, same representation mismatch as CSF, and no evidence it handles continuous aspect-ratio reshaping (only discrete rotation) — a materially weaker fit than for hard-macro benchmarks. **Payoff**: low. **Effort**: L. **Risk**: high (unverified numbers, representation mismatch).

**FAST-SP** — accelerates sequence-pair decode from O(n²) longest-path to O(n log log n) via a fast longest-common-subsequence-style computation, also natively supporting range/boundary placement constraints. Primary source: Tang & Wong, "FAST-SP: A fast algorithm for block placement based on sequence pair," ASP-DAC 2001, pp. 521–526, DOI 10.1145/370155.370523. Reported result: ami49 (n≈49) — better area (36.5 mm² vs 36.8 mm²) in 31s vs 4752s (~150× speedup) versus a prior B*-tree result, on 2001-era hardware. **Applicability**: confirmed **red herring at our scale** by two independent agents plus direct verification — at n<50, O(n²) decode is ~2500 operations, sub-millisecond, negligible next to a single `exactScore` call's Dijkstra-based routing cost. The bottleneck in our solver is exact routing evaluation, not decode. **Payoff**: negligible. **Effort**: S (if attempted). **Risk**: low but pointless — do not implement.

**MB*-tree** — two-stage clustering/declustering multilevel B*-tree framework: cluster modules by area-utilization/connectivity cost into sub-B*-trees, then iteratively decluster with local SA refinement, exploiting that B*-trees preserve geometric relations under decluster operations. Primary source: Chen, Hsu, Yang, Chang, DAC 2003 (IEEE Xplore 10.1109/1219130), extended as "MB*-Tree: A Multilevel Floorplanner for Large-Scale Building-Module Design," IEEE TCAD (full PDF at `cc.ee.ntu.edu.tw/~ywchang/Papers/tcad-mbtree.pdf`; binary PDF could not be text-extracted via `WebFetch`, so specific benchmark numbers are **unverified** from primary text, though the mechanism description is confirmed via abstract/secondary summaries). **Applicability**: confirmed **not beneficial at n<50** — the entire value proposition is amortizing SA search cost across hundreds-to-thousands of modules; clustering 50 blocks into ~10 super-blocks doesn't meaningfully shrink the search space relative to the bookkeeping overhead of cluster/decluster consistency. **Payoff**: none at our scale. **Effort**: L. **Risk**: high for zero expected gain — do not implement.

**Per-RMAP** — casts floorplanning as a feasibility-seeking problem solved by a generalized Method of Alternating Projections (MAP) with a resetting strategy (to escape MAP's divergence issues) plus "superiorization" perturbations that steer iterates toward lower wirelength without abandoning feasibility. Primary source: Yu, Censor, Jiang, Luo, "Per-RMAP: Feasibility-Seeking and Superiorization Methods for Floorplanning with I/O Assignment," ISEDA 2023, arXiv:2304.06698; successor "Floorplanning with I/O Assignment via Feasibility-Seeking and Superiorization Methods," arXiv:2406.03165 (2024), also on PMC (PMC11670884). Reported numbers (independently verified via direct fetch of the arXiv abstract page): legal floorplans "only two times slower than branch-and-bound" with "3% wirelength inferior to optimal" on MCNC benchmarks; with I/O assignment, an additional "8% improvement on wirelength." (Note: a separate secondary-source claim of "166× speedup / 5% HPWL improvement vs B&B, 15% speedup vs PeF" surfaced in one search snippet but could **not** be corroborated from the primary abstract text on direct fetch — treat that specific figure as unverified; the 2×-slower-than-B&B / 3%-worse-than-optimal figures are the ones confirmed directly from the source.) **Applicability**: conceptually adjacent to the GP/SOCP family below (both are convex/projection-based, not gradient-descent, not topology-search) — worth a follow-up deep-reasoner pass if convex soft-block shaping is pursued, but not independently verified in enough depth here to recommend on its own. **Payoff**: unclear, needs deeper read. **Effort**: M (unclear without a full read). **Risk**: medium — real but under-verified.

**F-FM** — two-stage analytical + slicing-tree approach for fixed-outline floorplanning of mixed-size modules under voltage-island constraints; explicitly extensible to routability/thermal objectives. Primary source: Lin & Wu, "F-FM: Fixed-Outline Floorplanning Methodology for Mixed-Size Modules Considering Voltage-Island Constraint," IEEE TCAD, vol. 33, no. 11, pp. 1681–1692, Nov. 2014 (confirmed real via direct search of the abstract — this resolves a prior "could not verify" flag from one research thread). **Applicability**: low direct transfer — voltage-island constraints and the slicing-tree-only representation don't map onto our non-slicing B*-tree/SP engines or our FT-conversion-table cost structure; noted here only for citation-accuracy closure, not as an actionable technique. **Payoff**: negligible. **Effort**: N/A. **Risk**: N/A.

### Thread 3 — Soft-block shaping

**SDS (Slack-Driven Shaping)** — optimal, scalable slack-driven algorithm for shaping soft blocks in non-slicing floorplans under a fixed-outline width bound: rather than heuristically nudging aspect ratios, SDS systematically distributes the *global* available slack across individual blocks so that layout height monotonically decreases and provably converges to an optimum, with two stated optimality conditions to check convergence. Primary source: (title/venue confirmed via multiple independent search hits) "Optimal Slack-Driven Block Shaping Algorithm in Fixed-Outline Floorplanning" / journal form "SDS: An Optimal Slack-Driven Block Shaping Algorithm for Fixed-Outline Floorplanning," ISPD 2012 (ACM DOI 10.1145/2160916.2160956; IEEE Xplore document 6416107), Napa, California, 2012-03-25/28, supported by an IBM Faculty Award and NSF. **Applicability**: directly relevant to `placement_engine.cpp`'s soft-block aspect-ratio mutation, which the internal cost-reduction-strategy report already flags as a weak "±15%-aspect-nudge SA move." SDS's slack-distribution logic is a principled, near-drop-in replacement for that heuristic nudge — it operates on a fixed topology (same precondition as our `decode` step) and targets exactly the fixed-outline-width-bound shape we already have via `outline_w`. **Payoff**: outline-area (SDS directly minimizes layout height under a width bound, which is exactly our shrink-wrap objective) and secondarily wirelength (better-shaped soft blocks reduce detour). **Effort**: M (needs adapting SDS's slack-distribution math to our specific soft-block set and FT-tier cost, since SDS's own objective doesn't natively know about FT-tier area inflation). **Risk**: medium — the paper's slack model doesn't include our tiered FT-area-inflation penalty; some adaptation work is required, but the core mechanism (distribute global slack, not uniform nudges) is a strict generalization of what we already attempt informally.

**Geometric Programming (GP) / SOCP shape-sizing** — given a *fixed* relative block arrangement (topology/adjacency), the width/height sizing subproblem becomes a posynomial (GP) or second-order-cone-representable (SOCP) program, solvable to global optimality after a log-change-of-variables (GP) or directly as a convex program (SOCP) — because "non-overlap given fixed ordering" reduces to linear/quadratic separation constraints. Primary sources: Moh, Chang, Hakimi, "Globally optimal floorplanning for a layout problem," *IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I*, Sept. 1996 (foundational GP formulation); Luo, Anjos, Vannelli, "A nonlinear optimization methodology for VLSI fixed-outline floorplanning," *Journal of Combinatorial Optimization*, DOI 10.1007/s10878-008-9148-y (2008) (SOCP-family reformulation; full text paywalled, so specific numbers are **unverified**, only title/venue/DOI/framing confirmed); related Waterloo dissertation by C. Luo, "Novel Convex Optimization Approaches for VLSI Floorplanning," covering the same author's SOCP line in more depth. Boyd & Vandenberghe's *Convex Optimization* (2004) is the standard reference for the GP→convex log-transform these build on. **Applicability**: this is the single most promising *new* soft-block-shaping lead found — as a post-`decode` (fixed-topology) co-optimization step, a GP/SOCP solve over all soft-block widths/heights jointly (rather than one aspect-ratio nudge at a time) directly targets the same weakness SDS targets, from a different mathematical angle (global convex optimum vs. slack-distribution heuristic — worth comparing both). The real risk, honestly assessed: our channel-overflow and FT-tier-mismatch penalties are step-function/combinatorial, not smooth convex functions of width/height, so expressing them in GP/SOCP-compatible form requires a convex relaxation or approximation whose fidelity to the true penalty would itself need validation — this is nontrivial modeling work, not a quick wrapper. A small permissively-licensed SOCP solver (ECOS, SCS) could be embedded and invoked periodically (e.g. alongside `--exact-interval`), not per-SA-move, given solver-call overhead. **Payoff**: outline-area primarily (tighter, jointly-optimal soft-block packing), secondarily wirelength. **Effort**: L (new convex-relaxation modeling work for our custom penalty terms, plus solver integration). **Risk**: medium-high — the payoff is real in principle but contingent on successfully relaxing our specific non-convex FT/channel penalties, which is the hard part.

### Thread 5 — Global routing on coarse graphs

**PathFinder-style negotiated congestion** — iterative rip-up-and-reroute where every net's edge cost combines a *present-congestion* term (how many nets currently share this resource) and a *history-cost* term (how often it has been congested across past iterations); all nets are rerouted each iteration under updated pricing until convergence, letting persistently-contested resources become progressively more expensive so losers detour. Primary source: McMurchie & Ebeling, "PathFinder: A Negotiation-Based Performance-Driven Router for FPGAs," FPGA'95, DOI 10.1145/201310.201328. Applied at ASIC **global-routing** granularity (not FPGA switchboxes — directly the abstraction level of our channel graph) by Roy & Markov, "High-Performance Routing at the Nanometer Scale," IEEE TCAD 27(6):1066–1077, 2008 (via router FGR), reporting −9.9%/−8.4% wirelength versus contest winners BoxRouter/MaizeRouter; and NTHU-Route 2.0 (Chang et al., ICCAD 2008), which eliminates overflow entirely on 7/8 ISPD07 and 12/16 ISPD08 benchmarks using the explicit edge cost `cost_e = b_e + h_e·p_e`. **Applicability**: the most concretely actionable *new* routing idea in this survey. Our bundles are already genuinely 2-terminal (no Steiner trees needed, consistent with PathFinder's own point-to-point examples), so this requires no representation change — only a structural addition to `routing.cpp`: wrap the existing per-bundle-chunk Dijkstra loop in an outer negotiation loop, add a `history_cost[channel]` state that increments for channels still over capacity after each full sweep, feed `cost += history_cost[channel] * present_usage_ratio` into the next sweep's edge weights, and repeat until stable or capped. This is compatible with the already-implemented P2 cost-driven-routing pass (Dijkstra weights = α·ΔWL + marginal area) — negotiation would add a temporal/iterative dimension on top of that per-edge pricing. Honest caveat: convergence is not proven in bounded iterations for arbitrary graphs (empirically ~30–50 rounds on FPGA benchmarks); each round re-runs the full per-chunk Dijkstra sweep, so cost scales as (negotiation rounds) × (current single-pass cost); and negotiation's marginal benefit over a single well-priced pass is *unproven* at our small scale (n<50 blocks, ≤8 chunks/bundle) — it matters more when many nets compete for the *same* scarce resource simultaneously in one shot, which is more characteristic of ISPD-scale routing than our per-bundle-chunk regime. Recommend only if channel overflow remains nontrivial after the existing cost-driven pass in practice. **Payoff**: wirelength/overflow reduction, secondarily outline-area (less forced channel widening). **Effort**: M (confined to `routing.cpp`, no new representation). **Risk**: medium — real engineering cost for an unproven-at-our-scale benefit; empirically test before committing.

**Min-cost multicommodity flow / Lagrangian channel pricing** — recasts global routing as flow with capacity-constrained arcs and per-commodity source-sink pairs, solved via LP relaxation + randomized rounding (Carden & Cheng, DAC'91/TCAD'96, using Shahrokhi-Matula's fractional multicommodity flow generalized via randomized rounding) or FPTAS-style combinatorial approximation with a certifiable LP-dual congestion bound (Albrecht, ISPD'00/TCAD'01, adapting Garg-Könemann and Fleischer schemes). Explicitly investigated and **rejected as over-engineering at this scale**: the randomized-rounding machinery in these papers exists specifically to handle *multi-pin* (Steiner-style) commodities, which is not our situation — every bundle here is a genuine single-pair commodity, so the LP/rounding overhead solves a harder problem than we have. At n<50 blocks with well under ~150 total graph nodes (blocks + channels) per chunk, an exact successive-shortest-path min-cost flow (not even LP relaxation) would be computationally trivial, but even that is heavier machinery than the existing greedy sequential Dijkstra-with-accrued-capacity-state approach, which is already a reasonable cheap approximation to the same congestion-pricing idea. **Important cross-finding**: Lagrangian capacity pricing (dualizing capacity constraints with iteratively-updated multipliers) is mathematically the *same object* as the LP-dual of this flow formulation, and is also the formally-principled version of what PathFinder's history-cost heuristically approximates without guarantees — treat flow-LP-duals and Lagrangian pricing as one research thread, not two, and prefer PathFinder's cheaper, empirically-validated approximation (Thread 5 above) over reimplementing either from scratch. **Payoff**: low marginal value over already-existing approach. **Effort**: L. **Risk**: high effort-to-benefit ratio — not recommended.

**Contest-winning floorplanners with public code, general search**: beyond `iccad-2023-problem-D`, no other confirmed contest-winning floorplanner repos were found for ICCAD/ISPD floorplanning contests specifically (searches returned only `IntelLabs/FloorSet`, which is a dataset not a solver, and unrelated academic repos).

### Thread 7 — SA meta-optimization

**Honesty flag up front**: three of the five sub-areas researched here (parallel tempering, adaptive move-type weighting, wall-clock-bounded framing) have **no floorplanning-specific primary source** — every citation is transplanted from general combinatorial optimization, scheduling, or evolutionary-algorithm literature. This is stated explicitly rather than forcing a fit, per the task's own instructions.

**Parallel tempering / replica exchange** — multiple SA chains at different temperatures periodically attempt Metropolis-criterion state swaps, letting high-temperature exploration migrate down to low-temperature refinement. Primary sources: Almeida, de Castro Lima & Carvalho, *Computers & Operations Research*, Feb 2025 (general OR, CPU-parallel, three case studies, specific numbers blocked by a 403 on full text); Cicirello, "Variable Annealing Length and Parallelism in Simulated Annealing," SoCS 2017 / arXiv:1709.02877 (scheduling, not floorplanning, but the most quantitatively concrete data found). **No VLSI-floorplanning-specific PT primary source exists** — searched and confirmed absent. Cicirello's directly-relevant finding: periodic best-solution sharing across parallel SA chains showed **no statistically significant benefit** over fully independent chains on a comparably-sized (N=8 chains, 60s budget) combinatorial scheduling benchmark, except marginally in the run's final 15–20%. **Applicability**: this is a caution flag, not a green light — our `hybrid` engine is currently sequential, so PT would be new infrastructure (swap logic across sp-permutation and bstar-tree representations at different temperatures is nontrivial), and the one piece of hard evidence available argues against expecting a reliable win from cross-chain information sharing versus the already-planned independent-multi-start "portfolio" approach. **Payoff**: unproven, possibly none. **Effort**: L. **Risk**: medium-high — do not prioritize over the portfolio approach already in the roadmap.

**Adaptive move-type probability tuning** — reweight which move type (swap/rotate/resize/relocate) gets proposed based on recent acceptance/improvement statistics, framed as a dynamic multi-armed bandit credit-assignment problem. Primary source (evolutionary-algorithm operator selection, not SA, not floorplanning): DaCosta, Fialho, Schoenauer, Sebag, "Adaptive Operator Selection with Dynamic Multi-Armed Bandits," GECCO 2008, DOI 10.1145/1389095.1389272; extended in Fialho et al., *Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence*, 2010. **No SA-specific or floorplanning-specific primary source exists for this exact mechanism** — a promising-looking lead (Xu, Wang, Deng, "The Impact of Move Schemes on Simulated Annealing Performance," arXiv:2504.17949, April 2025) was checked in full and found to be about **continuous** optimization (Lennard-Jones clusters, Rosenbrock function), studying how many *coordinates* to perturb per move, not discrete move-*type* selection — does not satisfy the ask despite the promising title. **Applicability**: the DaCosta/Fialho mechanism (per-move-type credit assignment via UCB1-style dynamic reweighting) is directly transplantable to `placement_engine.cpp`'s `mutate` — instrument move-type accept-rate and mean-`ΔcheapScore` over a sliding window, feed into a UCB selector — with no architectural change to the SA loop itself. **Payoff**: potentially better search efficiency (more proposals spent on productive move types). **Effort**: S-M (self-contained, isolated to `mutate`). **Risk**: high in the sense of *unvalidated* — this is a plausible-but-unproven transplant from EA literature with zero SA-specific precedent found; treat as a cheap, easily-A/B-testable experiment against fixed move probabilities on the existing regression cases, not a load-bearing roadmap commitment.

**Elite-pool sophistication** — diversity-aware elite-pool management (balancing quality against diversity, not pure top-K by score) and B*-tree crossover operators for SA. Primary sources: Jaradat, Ayob, Almarashdeh, "The effect of elite pool in hybrid population-based meta-heuristics for solving combinatorial optimization problems," *Applied Soft Computing*, 44:45–56, 2016 (general combinatorial optimization: TSP, 0-1 multidimensional knapsack, capacitated VRP — finds diversity-aware elite pools outperform simple retention across instances and problem classes, directional claim, exact percentages not extracted from paywalled full text); Shunmugathammal, Columbus, Anand, "A Novel B*-tree Crossover-Based Simulated Annealing Algorithm (BCSA) for Combinatorial Optimization in VLSI Fixed-Outline Floorplans," *Circuits, Systems, and Signal Processing*, 2020, DOI 10.1007/s00034-019-01054-9 (floorplanning-specific, MCNC benchmarks, reported as outperforming most SOTA comparisons but specific percentages blocked by paywall). **Applicability**: our existing "final pass exact-scores top-16 cheap candidates plus origin-compacted variants" is precisely a simple top-K pool; Jaradat et al.'s directional finding supports a small, low-risk upgrade (add a diversity criterion — e.g. dedupe by `placementKey` distance, not just score, before taking the top-16) using infrastructure that already exists. BCSA-style crossover between elite B*-trees is a larger, genuinely GA-shaped lift requiring a valid-tree-preserving crossover operator on `BStarEngine` — floorplanning-specific evidence exists for it, but the reported margin over baseline SA isn't quantified in what's accessible. **Payoff**: diversity-aware selection — small but well-supported wirelength/area gains; BCSA crossover — potentially larger but unquantified. **Effort**: S (diversity-aware top-K) / L (B*-tree crossover). **Risk**: low-medium (diversity) / medium (crossover — new capability on `BStarEngine`).

**CPU-only multithreaded SA thread-count scaling** — how solution quality scales with thread count at fixed wall-clock budget. Primary sources: Fang, Chang, Chen, Liang, Hsieh, Satria, Han, "A Parallel Simulated Annealing Approach for Floorplanning in VLSI," ICA3PP 2009, Springer LNCS 5574, pp. 291–302, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-03095-6_29 (floorplanning-specific, OpenMP-based, but full-text thread-count-vs-quality curves paywalled — only the qualitative "better utilization, less time" abstract claim is accessible); Cicirello (SoCS 2017, cited above) provides the only quantified scaling curve found, on a comparably-sized scheduling problem: **N=4 chains → only 2-3× speedup (not 4×); N=8 chains → only ~4× (not 8×)** — direct evidence of sublinear scaling even with zero inter-chain communication, intrinsic to SA's "one long run beats many short runs" property; PARSAC (already known, arXiv:2405.05495) reports absolute wall-clock numbers at a single fixed worker count (112 cores) but **no diminishing-returns curve was found** in accessible text. **Applicability**: given 64 available cores but n<50 blocks and a small per-iteration state, expect diminishing (not catastrophic) returns well before 64-way SA-chain parallelism — the binding constraint is more likely the search-space size itself (many chains converge to similar local optima quickly at this scale) than hardware contention. This argues for a **moderate SA worker count (8–16)** with remaining cores reallocated to routing/eval parallelism (Dijkstra-per-bundle and channel extraction are more embarrassingly parallel and directly cheapen the expensive exact-eval tier) rather than pushing SA-chain-count to 64. **Payoff**: sets a concrete expectation (budget ~2-4× quality gain from 4-8 chains, not more) for the already-planned portfolio approach — this is calibration value, not a new technique. **Effort**: M (building the N-worker portfolio, already anticipated in the roadmap). **Risk**: medium — Cicirello's number is from a different problem domain, treat as a planning prior, not a guarantee.

**Wall-clock-bounded (not iteration-bounded) SA framing** — papers explicitly measuring SA performance against elapsed time rather than iteration count, matching the contest's 2-hour aggregate budget. **No 2023–2026 paper combining SA + floorplanning/VLSI-CAD + explicit wall-clock framing was found** — despite targeted searches ("anytime simulated annealing floorplanning," "time-bounded simulated annealing," ICCAD/DAC/ISPD 2024–2025 wall-clock searches). The best available source remains Cicirello (SoCS 2017)'s Variable Annealing Length (VAL) schedule — an exponentially-increasing restart-length schedule designed for exactly the "unknown available time budget" problem our solver faces when allocating `--iters`/`--exact-interval` per case without knowing case count/difficulty in advance ahead of time. **Applicability**: directly transplantable to `search.cpp`'s `runPlacementSearch` — restructure the fixed iteration-count parameter into a restart schedule, no representation change needed. **Payoff**: this is a robustness/variance-reduction lever ("stop getting a much worse score when a testcase runs long or short relative to expectations"), not a mean-quality-improvement lever — should be scoped and evaluated as such. **Effort**: S. **Risk**: low, given the hard aggregate-2h constraint this is cheap insurance regardless of whether it improves mean quality.

## Top-10 ranked recommendations

Ranked by payoff/effort for reducing `Cost = OutlineArea + α·TotalWireLength`, given everything above and the existing P0–P4 roadmap already in `2026-07-06-cost-reduction-strategy.md`.

1. **SDS-style slack-driven soft-block shaping** (ISPD 2012) — replace the ±15%-aspect-nudge SA move with global-slack distribution across all soft blocks at once; targets outline-area directly under the fixed-width-bound objective we already have. **M effort, principled math, direct fit to an already-flagged weakness.**
2. **Diversity-aware elite-pool selection** (Jaradat et al. 2016) — add a diversity criterion to the existing top-16 elite pass; uses infrastructure already in place. **S effort, low risk, directionally well-supported.**
3. **PathFinder-style negotiated congestion in `routing.cpp`** (McMurchie & Ebeling 1995, via Roy & Markov 2008 / NTHU-Route 2.0) — outer rip-up/history-cost loop around the existing per-bundle Dijkstra, composable with the already-implemented P2 cost-driven pass. **M effort; test empirically before committing, since benefit at n<50 is unproven but the ceiling (−9.9% WL in the cited modern application) is meaningful if it transfers.**
4. **Port OpenROAD's RUDY congestion estimator** into `eval.cpp`'s `cheapScore` congestion proxy — concrete, small, permissively-licensed (BSD-3-Clause), standalone. **S-M effort, low risk, improves proxy fidelity → better SA acceptance decisions.**
5. **Variable-Annealing-Length wall-clock-robust iteration schedule** (Cicirello 2017) — restructure `search.cpp`'s fixed `--iters` into a restart schedule responsive to actual elapsed time under the 2h aggregate budget. **S effort, insurance against budget misallocation across testcases, not a mean-quality lever but cheap and safe.**
6. **Adaptive move-type selection via dynamic MAB** (DaCosta/Fialho 2008) — instrument `mutate`'s move-type acceptance/improvement statistics, feed a UCB1 selector. **S-M effort, self-contained, easy A/B test, but zero SA-specific precedent — treat as an experiment, verify before trusting.**
7. **GP/SOCP fixed-topology soft-block co-optimization** (Moh/Chang/Hakimi 1996; Luo/Anjos/Vannelli 2008) — joint convex solve over all soft-block dimensions post-`decode`, as a periodic (not per-move) step. **L effort — the hard part is convex-relaxing our step-function FT/channel penalties — but potentially the highest-ceiling outline-area lever found if that relaxation succeeds.**
8. **Read `johnnyhuang1007/iccad-2023-problem-D`'s raw C++ source** directly (MIT-licensed, not yet actually read past the README) — a real contest-winning fixed-outline solver for a closely-related (rectilinear soft blocks) problem. **S effort (just reading), payoff unknown until done — cheap due diligence.**
9. **Per-RMAP / feasibility-seeking as an alternative convex-adjacent soft-shaping method** (Yu/Censor/Jiang/Luo 2023/2024) — worth a deeper read alongside #7 since it's a different (projection-based, not gradient-based) angle on the same convex-shaping opportunity; may be cheaper to implement than full GP/SOCP tooling. **M effort pending a fuller read; do this investigation before committing to #7's larger build.**
10. **B*-tree crossover SA (BCSA)** (Shunmugathammal et al. 2020) — floorplanning-specific evidence for elite-pool crossover beyond simple diversity-aware retention (#2), but requires a genuinely new valid-tree-preserving crossover operator on `BStarEngine`. **L effort, medium risk, do only after #2 is exhausted and if elite-pool improvements are still wanted.**

**Explicitly not recommended** (researched and rejected, with reasons, to avoid future re-investigation): FAST-SP (decode speed is irrelevant at n<50), MB*-tree (needs hundreds of modules to pay off), min-cost multicommodity flow / Lagrangian channel pricing (over-engineering — the existing sequential Dijkstra with accrued capacity state already approximates the same congestion-pricing idea more cheaply), Dai & Kuh's BEAR channel ordering (geometry mismatch — nested slicing channels vs. our flat vertical-slice channels, same reason STAIRoute/HGR/CUGR were already rejected), CSF/CSAQ and FFA-CD/FA-GSS as primary engines (representation mismatch, hard-modules-only or unclear soft-block support), parallel tempering (one hard data point argues cross-chain information-sharing doesn't reliably help at comparable problem scale — prefer the already-planned independent portfolio).
